01 // SCIENTIFIC FOCUS

Research Pillars & Methodologies

Combining multi-wavelength observations from Indian and international facilities with Bayesian inference to probe ultra-dense nuclear matter and the nanohertz gravitational wave sky.

Pulsar Timing Irregularities & Neutron Star Interiors

Investigating rotational glitches and red timing noise in young and millisecond pulsars to constrain the internal structure of neutron stars, superfluid vortex unpinning, fractional moment of inertia, and two-fluid equation-of-state models. Lead author on post-glitch recovery analysis of the Vela Pulsar (PASA 2026) and Gaussian Process timing noise realisations (PASA 2024).

Post-Glitch Recovery Superfluid Interior Automated Glitch Pipeline Vela & PSR J1740-3015

Pulsar Timing Arrays & Gravitational Wave Background

Active member of the Indian Pulsar Timing Array (InPTA) and International Pulsar Timing Array (IPTA). Contributing to precision wideband timing, single-pulsar customized noise budgets, profile reconstruction from temporally stable emission components (PSR J1713+0747), and searches for the Stochastic Gravitational Wave Background (InPTA DR2 & DR3).

InPTA DR2 & DR3 Stochastic GW Background Wideband Timing Pulse Profile Reconstruction

Radio & X-Ray Observational Campaigns & Instrumentation

Principal Investigator on 17 uGMRT observation proposals and Co-PI on 8 proposals (Cycles 41–51) alongside high-cadence Ooty Radio Telescope (ORT) monitoring and AstroSat X-ray (LAXPC/CZTI) timing of the Crab Pulsar. Coordinated construction of a 21 cm H I Pyramidal Horn Radio Telescope and an 11.2 GHz motorized small radio telescope at IIT Roorkee.

uGMRT (17 PI / 8 Co-PI) Ooty Radio Telescope AstroSat LAXPC & CZTI 21 cm Horn Telescope

Bayesian Modelling, Gaussian Processes & Machine Learning

Applying Gaussian Process regression to model stochastic timing irregularities, hierarchical Bayesian inference for pulsar glitch recovery timescales and single-pulsar noise analysis, multivariate pulsar population studies, and machine-learning pipelines for automated glitch detection and γ-ray detector calibration.

Bayesian Inference Gaussian Processes Automated ML Pipelines SKAO Science Working Group

Portfolio Design Recommendations & GitHub Pages Guide

1. Single-Page vs. Multiple Pages — What works best for Astrophysics Researchers?

A Smart Single-Page Application with Sticky Section Navigation & Interactive Filters (like this site) is strongly preferred by faculty search committees, postdoc hosts, and collaborators over separate multi-page clicks:

  • Zero Page-Reload Friction: Reviewers can scan your Research Pillars, 20 Publications, 25 uGMRT proposals, and ISRO Award in 60 seconds without hunting through sub-menus.
  • Interactive Filtering Instead of Clutter: Your 20 publications are organized with instant filter tabs (All, Journal Articles, Lead-Author Highlights, ATels, Proceedings) and a live search bar so the page stays compact.
  • Instant GitHub Maintenance: Having everything in one self-contained index.html file means updating a new paper or ATel takes 30 seconds.

2. Why a Live Pulsar Telemetry & Glitch Simulator Beats Generic "Snowfall"

Generic snow/particle templates can sometimes reduce text readability or feel unrelated to your specific science. Instead, this website features:

  • Interactive Pulsar Glitch Simulator (Hero Section): Visitors can click between actual pulsars from your papers (Vela PSR J0835-4510, PSR J1740-3015, MSP J1713+0747, Crab Pulsar), watch the rotating lighthouse beam and folded radio sub-pulses, and click "Trigger Spin-Up Glitch (Δν/ν)" to watch a phase jump and exponential post-glitch recovery in real time!
  • Multi-Layer Deep-Space Starfield & Nebula Canvas: Uses realistic stellar spectral colors (O/B blue-white to K/M orange) with a top-bar toggle (Pulsar Field / Minimal Starfield / Static Dark) so readers who prefer a static background can switch with one click.
